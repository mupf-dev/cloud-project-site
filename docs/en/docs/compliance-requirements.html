Compliance requirements

Which regulatory frameworks apply to DCP, how the requirements catalog is structured, how obligations are split between platform, operator and tenant, and which precedence rules apply in case of conflicts.

Note: This page presents a technical requirements analysis and is not legal advice. Research status: November 2026. Binding use requires checking against the original documents.

Key points#

  1. It is the operator that gets certified or attested, not the software. The platform must enable operators to implement controls effectively and provide evidence for them. To that end it delivers technical functions, secure defaults, a shared responsibility matrix and exportable evidence.
  2. The software itself is subject to the EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA) – for anyone who places it on the market in the course of a commercial activity. Reporting obligations have applied since 2026-09-11, all other obligations from 2027-12-11. Parts of the platform (OS image, identity management, hypervisor and container runtime) are expected to fall under "important products".
  3. BSI C5:2026 has been published – with 168 basic criteria in 17 areas and new focus topics such as container management, supply chain/SBOM, post-quantum cryptography and confidential computing. It becomes mandatory for audits from mid-2027.
  4. Grundschutz++ has been published as BSI Standard 300-1 since 2026-10-26. The user catalog with around 1,000 requirements is available in machine-readable OSCAL format. Classic BSI IT-Grundschutz remains usable for a transition period of several years.
  5. NIS2 has applied in Germany since 2025-12-06. Cloud providers and data center operators are among the NIS2 sectors; for them, an EU implementing regulation specifies the technical measures.
  6. Lead catalog: ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A provides the structure, BSI C5:2026 is the benchmark for content, Grundschutz++ the machine-readable source of detail. In case of conflict the stricter requirement applies – as long as it remains configurable for all deployment sizes from homelab to data center.

Structure of the requirements catalog#

Every requirement has a stable ID of the form REQ-<Block>-<Nr>, a wording as a requirement on the platform, its sources (ISO, C5, BSI IT-Grundschutz, Grundschutz++, CRA, NIS2, GDPR, technical guidelines), a priority and the affected components.

Priorities: MVP = met with the first working version · P2 = before the first production deployment in a data center · P3 = later or optional.

Components: agent, control plane, identity, API/portal/MCP, boot, OS image, compute, storage, network, KMS, observability, audit log, build/CI.

Shared responsibility#

Principle: The platform provides capabilities, secure defaults and evidence data. The operator applies them, organizes, decides and is accountable for the ISMS. The tenant is responsible for its workloads and data. The attestation or certificate is always issued to the operator.

Homelab: Without a compliance goal the same defaults apply; operator obligations become recommendations. No function may be secure only with data center infrastructure (HSM, SIEM, second site) – every such dependency needs a secure fallback, for example Shamir secret sharing instead of an HSM, or local log storage instead of a SIEM.

Lead catalog#

  1. Backbone: ISO/IEC 27001 Annex A (supplemented by 27017 for cloud). Every requirement is first mapped to an Annex A control – every ISMS builds on it.
  2. Benchmark for content: BSI C5:2026. Where C5 is more specific or stricter – tenant isolation, access to customer data, product security, transparency, containers, post-quantum cryptography – C5 determines the acceptance criteria. Goal: an operator should be able to meet all technical C5 basic criteria with the platform.
  3. Source of detail and machine-readable data: Grundschutz++. The catalog is imported directly and provides concrete check items (see BSI IT-Grundschutz modeling).
  4. Legal minimum: CRA for the software, NIS2 for operators. Legal requirements are not open to trade-offs.

Conflict rules#

RuleContent
K1 – Law before standardsCRA, NIS2/BSIG and GDPR take precedence over any standard.
K2 – Stricter winsIf C5, ISO and Grundschutz++ differ, the stricter requirement applies as the platform default – provided it remains configurable.
K3 – Configurability instead of coercionWhere strictness makes homelab operation impossible, it is offered as a profile, with a documented secure fallback. Two axes: deployment profile (homelab, standard-c5, erhoeht – "elevated") and crypto profile (bsi-strict as default, compatible); the regulated deployment profiles only with bsi-strict.
K4 – Cryptography per BSIFor cryptographic questions BSI TR-02102 is authoritative; deviations are justified and documented.
K5 – VerifiabilityA requirement counts as implemented only when the platform delivers automatically generated evidence.
K6 – Version disciplineReferences carry their version; new editions are assessed as a delta, transition periods determine the priority.

There is no real precedence conflict between C5 and ISO 27001: ISO 27001 is the framework, C5:2026 the stricter, cloud-specific refinement. How these requirements are already anchored in the architecture is described in Compliance by design.
